Actionable Recommendations

Specific plays for AE, SDR, SE, and Marketing teams

AE 60-Min Outreach Rule

23.5% of no-shows are closed within 24h of booking — the lead went cold before ever being contacted. Enforce a hard 60-minute first-contact SLA from demo booking for all Marketing MQLs. Tooling: Salesforce alert + Slack ping.

AE APAC: Stop Holding Open Opps

APAC AEs hold no-show opps 16.3 days on average before closing — nearly 2x EMEA. Set a hard rule: if no contact is made within 5 days of a no-show, mark Closed Lost. This frees AE calendar and improves pipeline accuracy.

SDR Pre-Demo Confirmation Sequence

Only 86 of 601 no-shows (14%) had SDR involvement. Build a 3-touch pre-demo confirmation sequence: T-48h email, T-24h SMS/WhatsApp, T-2h call. SDR-assisted opps show higher show rates. Prioritise this for high-value lead sources (Events, AI Referrals, Referrals).

  • T-48h: Personalised confirmation email with agenda
  • T-24h: WhatsApp/SMS reminder with join link
  • T-2h: SDR call — rescheduling offer if unavailable

SDR Same-Day No-Show Recovery

When a no-show is flagged (Zoom no-join signal), SDR has a 4-hour window to re-engage. Script: "Looks like we missed you — happy to do a quick 10-min call now or reschedule." Studies show 30–40% of no-shows will reschedule if contacted within 2 hours.

Marketing Retire Legacy Campaigns

EOR_Hiring_AM1_S_Sep_2021 alone drove 48 no-shows. Audit all campaigns older than 18 months for no-show rate, not just lead volume. Pause or restructure campaigns where no-show rate exceeds 15%. Prioritise GP India and legacy EMEA EOR campaigns immediately.

Marketing Add a Qualification Gate

The top lead_source is "Demo Booked" — no pre-qualification friction. Consider adding a 2-question qualifier on the Book a Demo page: hiring timeline + headcount. Route anything below threshold to a nurture sequence instead of a live demo slot. This reduces volume but improves show rate.

Ops No-Show Rate as a Campaign KPI

No-show rate is not currently tracked as a first-class marketing KPI. Add it to the weekly Brand KPI dashboard alongside MQL volume. Target: <10% no-show rate per campaign. Alert when any campaign exceeds 15% in a rolling 4-week window.

Ops Mobile-Booked Demo Risk Scoring

Mobile bookings show 193 no-shows (32% of total). These leads often book impulsively. Consider a lighter-touch engagement path for mobile bookings — short video demo or async Loom instead of a synchronous meeting. Test with APAC and EMEA first.

Key Findings

What the data is actually saying

Paid Search has a structural problem, not a bad month

At 32.5%, nearly 1 in 3 Paid Search MQLs never attends a demo. That rate held between 31% and 44% across every month from January through April. May dropped to 19%, which looks encouraging, but May is still incomplete. The consistency of the problem across five months rules out seasonality. Something about who these campaigns attract is fundamentally misaligned with demo intent.

Other Campaigns spiked to 60% in February then swung to near zero

The volatility in Other Campaigns (60% in Feb, 0% in Mar, 36% in Apr, 4% in May) signals inconsistent lead quality rather than a broken channel. With only 82 total MQLs, the rate swings on just a handful of records. This is a volume problem as much as a quality problem. The February spike in particular warrants a look at exactly which campaigns fed into that month.

AI Referrals opened badly and is stabilising

January came in at 43%, which was alarming. From February onwards the rate settled into the 7 to 20% range. This looks like a new channel warming up with early unqualified traffic. Volume is growing (29 MQLs in May vs 21 in Jan) so the trend direction matters. If the rate stays below 15% as volume scales, AI Referrals is worth investing in. If it creeps back up, the targeting brief needs tightening.

Organic Search improved sharply and is now at an acceptable rate

Organic ran at 17 to 18% in the first three months, then dropped to 6% in April and held there in May. That is a meaningful shift. It could reflect a change in which pages are driving demo bookings, or improved keyword ranking for higher-intent queries. Worth understanding what changed in March or April that caused this improvement so it can be reinforced, not accidentally undone.

Events and Webinars are the cleanest sources and are being underinvested

Events at 1.6% and Webinars at 3.6% are not flukes. These leads have already raised their hand twice before reaching a demo. They registered, they attended, and then they booked. That is a fundamentally different intent signal than clicking an ad. Events generated 251 MQLs this year. Scaling that channel even marginally could have an outsized impact on overall show rates.

The improvement trend across sources from February to May is real, but May is incomplete

Almost every source shows its lowest no-show rate in April or May. That is encouraging. However, May only has data through roughly the 26th, and no-shows from demos booked late in the month may not have been marked yet. Treat May rates as directional until the month fully closes. The more reliable signal is the April rate, which also showed broad improvement across Paid Search, Organic, and Direct Traffic.

Key Findings

What the three-way cross is telling us

Region is not the driver. Source mix explains the regional gap.

NAMER looks worst at 26.9% and EMEA looks best at 17.9%, but that gap largely comes from NAMER carrying more Paid Search volume and more AOR and GP campaigns. When you hold source and product constant, the regional differences shrink. Fixing Paid Search AOR and GP campaigns fixes NAMER more than any region-specific intervention would.

GP through Paid Search is the highest-rate problem across all three regions

NAMER Paid Search GP sits at 53%, APAC at 44%, EMEA at 34%. Every region running GP campaigns through Paid Search is producing no-shows at a rate that cannot be justified by downstream show-rate optimisation. The leads themselves are the wrong ones. GP search terms likely attract people who are price-checking payroll costs rather than evaluating a vendor. The fix is at the campaign brief and keyword targeting level, not the AE outreach level.

AOR through Paid Search is a volume and rate problem at the same time

NAMER Paid Search AOR generated 61 no-shows at 40%, APAC at 38%, EMEA at 40%. That is 119 wasted demo slots from a single source-product combination across three regions. AOR has a narrower ICP than EOR and the Paid Search audience is almost certainly too broad for it. A tighter negative keyword list and stricter audience segmentation would have immediate impact on this number.

EOR through Paid Search is a volume problem, not a rate problem

EOR no-show rates through Paid Search sit between 22% and 30% across regions. High in absolute terms, but significantly better than AOR and GP. Given EOR is the core product with the broadest market fit, some level of no-show waste is expected. The priority is improving the 30% NAMER rate rather than pulling back on the channel entirely.

Non-paid sources are clean across all products and regions

Organic Search, Direct Traffic, Events, and Webinars consistently sit below 15% regardless of product or region. EMEA Organic EOR at 7% across 227 MQLs is the standout. These are people who sought out Multiplier directly rather than being intercepted by an ad. The intent signal is fundamentally different and it shows in the show rates. This is the benchmark to optimise paid acquisition toward.

Other Campaigns has a small but severe GP problem worth auditing

APAC Other Campaigns GP produced 5 no-shows from only 6 MQLs and NAMER Other Campaigns GP was 75% (6 from 8). These are tiny volumes but they signal that whatever campaigns are being lumped into Other Campaigns for GP are completely missing on audience. Worth identifying exactly which campaigns sit in this bucket for the GP product before Q3 planning.

Mobile bookings are a 6x no-show risk signal

34.8% of no-shows booked on mobile versus 5.8% of good MQLs among known-device records. Someone booking a demo on their phone is far more likely to be in a passive browsing state rather than an active buying mode. They book in a moment of interest but have no real intent to attend. Desktop bookings split almost identically across both cohorts (64.5% vs 67.6%), confirming this is not a device quality problem — it is a booking context problem. A mobile-specific confirmation flow or a soft reschedule prompt sent immediately after a mobile booking could meaningfully shift show rates.

Below 20 employees: no-shows are overrepresented by 13pp

1-20 employee companies make up 45.9% of the no-show known pool but only 32.8% of the good MQL pool. These are likely early-stage or micro-businesses where the person booking the demo is also running payroll, operations, and five other things. Demo intent is real but follow-through is low because the buyer has no dedicated time to evaluate vendors properly.

50-100 employees: the clearest ICP signal in the data

This band is 15.7% of good MQLs but only 7.5% of no-shows — a 8.2pp gap and the largest positive signal for what a good buyer looks like. Companies in the 50 to 100 range are large enough to have dedicated HR or finance functions evaluating EOR and payroll vendors, but small enough that a single decision-maker can act on a demo. This is where targeting should be concentrated.

Email type is not a screening signal

97.7% of no-shows used a business email. So did 96.7% of good MQLs. The 1pp gap is not actionable. The hypothesis that personal email addresses indicate low-quality or fraudulent intent does not hold for this cohort. These are real people at real companies — they are just not ready to buy when they book the demo, particularly when they arrive from Paid Search on mobile at a very small company.

The verdict: it is both. Wrong person at small companies, right person on mobile but wrong moment.

The data splits into two distinct failure modes. Under-20-employee companies booking via Paid Search on GP or AOR campaigns represent the wrong person — the ICP is too small and the product-market fit is weak for that segment. Mobile bookings from appropriately-sized companies represent the right person at the wrong moment — they have real intent but are not in a context where they will follow through on a scheduled call. These two failure modes need different interventions. The first needs a targeting and qualification gate fix. The second needs a booking flow and confirmation sequence fix.

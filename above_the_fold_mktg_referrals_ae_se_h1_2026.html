What worked, what did not

Marketing + Referrals, H1 2026

What worked

  • Pipeline supply held up. SQL volume grew 14% and SQL pipeline grew 32% QoQ. Top of funnel is not the constraint.
  • EMEA AE closing rate jumped in June to 36% SQL→Win, the strongest single cell in H1.
  • Referrals revenue spiked in Q2 on a handful of larger wins, lifting the blended revenue number.
  • APAC SE punch above weight. On a real base of 80 SQLs they out-convert APAC AEs on both metrics.

What did not

  • Wins went flat (+4%) while pipeline grew 32%. The funnel is leaking at the close.
  • Marketing Pipe→Rev fell from 19.7% to 15.3%. Marketing is 87% of revenue, so this is the biggest absolute drag.
  • SE pipeline share shrank from 21% to 17% of SQLs, concentrating more volume on the AE bench.
  • Referrals win count dropped 30 to 20 even as revenue rose, a thin and volatile base for Q3 planning.

Recommendations for Q3

Prescriptive, ranked by expected revenue impact

1. Fix Marketing close efficiency before adding pipeline

Marketing Pipe→Rev dropped 4.4 points QoQ on the largest revenue base. Recovering Q1 efficiency on the current Q2 pipeline is worth more than any routing change. Diagnose the AE bench: capacity, deal quality, or stage hygiene.

Largest absolute leverOwner: Sales leadership + RevOps

2. Run a controlled APAC SE reallocation test

APAC routes 74% of marketing SQLs to AEs, yet APAC SEs convert pipeline to revenue far more efficiently on a non-trivial base. Move a defined slice of APAC marketing SQLs to SE and measure SQL→Win over a full quarter. This is the clearest mis-routing signal in the data.

Highest-confidence routing testOwner: APAC Sales Manager

3. Keep NAMER AE-led

The hypothesis does not hold in NAMER. AEs out-convert SEs on both SQL→Win and Pipe→Rev, so the AE-heavy routing there is correct. Do not reallocate NAMER pipeline to SE on the strength of the aggregate number.

Hold, do not changeOwner: NAMER Sales

4. Treat Referrals revenue as volatile, not a trend

The Q2 referrals jump is a few large deals on a tiny base. Plan Q3 referrals revenue off win count and a multi-quarter average, not the Q2 dollar figure.

Planning hygieneOwner: Partnerships + Analytics

Bottom line. The Q2 revenue lag is a conversion problem, not a pipeline problem. Q3 targets are steep, and more SQLs alone will not close the gap. The two highest-value moves are recovering Marketing close efficiency and testing a measured APAC SE reallocation. NAMER stays as is.

Method. Source table gtm.full_funnel_details, funnel_date in Jan–Jun 2026. Marketing = grouped_source 'Marketing'; Referrals = grouped_source 'Affiliates' (per scope). AE vs SE is parsed from the raw role text (role_category collapses both into "AE" and cannot be used). SE = roles tagged (SE), "Sales Executive", or a standalone SE token; AE = (AE), "Account Executive", or a standalone AE token. AE/SE-mixed, BDR, SDR, CSM, partnerships and null roles are grouped as Other and excluded from the AE vs SE comparison.

Metric definitions. SQL and Win are row counts at those funnel stages. Pipeline is SUM(pipeline) on SQL rows. Revenue is SUM(revenue) on Revenue rows. SQL→Win and Pipe→Rev are within-period ratios, not opportunity-level cohorts, so a Q2 win may originate from a Q1 SQL. SE sample sizes are small and dollar ratios are sensitive to individual deals; gaps are directional, not tested for significance.

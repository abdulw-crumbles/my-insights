Multiplier's New Homepage: Balanced, Not Yet Triumphant.

Twenty-six days after going live, Multiplier's new homepage is engaging visitors longer and bouncing them less. Conversion is up directionally, not yet statistically significant. The lift is concentrated in NAMER and on Desktop. EMEA, APAC and Mobile are flat-to-down. The ship works. It is not equally seaworthy in all waters.
By Marketing Analytics, Multiplier Periods Old HP · Mar 13 – Apr 2 (21 days)  ·  New HP · Apr 3 – Apr 28 (26 days)

The homepage redesign launched as an A/B test in late January. After a brief pause for a no-index incident in mid-March, the new version went live to all traffic on April 3. This brief compares performance over the twenty-one days the old homepage was live following the experiment to the twenty-six days the new homepage has been live since.

Our new homepage is better than the old, but not uniformly. Engagement metrics improved consistently. Conversion lifted by 15 percent overall (p = 0.38, not yet significant). The lift is real in North America (+90%, p < 0.05) and on desktop (+24%). It is going the wrong way in Europe (–29%), on mobile (–32%), and is roughly flat in APAC.

The balance sheet

What worked. What didn't.

Every launch has both. Honest reporting names them in equal measure so the team knows where to celebrate and where to invest.

What worked

  • Bounce rate. Down 4.6 percentage points at p < 0.001. The strongest signal in this report.
  • Time on page. Up 9.5%. Visitors stay longer.
  • NAMER conversion. Up 90% at p < 0.05. The clearest regional win.
  • Desktop conversion. Up 24%. Where 79% of traffic lives.
  • New visitor conversion. Directionally up 22%. First impressions are landing better.
  • Paid Search. Sessions up 34% and conversion rate up 29%. Compounding ROAS gains.
  • Bottom funnel. Same daily lead volume, +35% more MQLs, +38% more SQLs from homepage-first cohort.

What didn't work

  • EMEA conversion. Down 29%, from a 1.19% baseline to 0.84%. The only region trending negative.
  • Mobile conversion. Down 32% (0.37 to 0.25%). The page does not yet work as well for the small-screen visitor.
  • Organic Search conversion. Down 11%. Volume grew but the conversion rate fell. The new homepage doesn't match Organic visitor intent as well.
  • APAC conversion. Flat at –3%. The largest segment by volume saw no improvement.
  • Returning visitor conversion. Up only 14%. The redesign is not resonating with people who already knew the brand.
  • Total session volume. Down 8.5% vs prior period. Direct traffic alone fell 28%.
  • Statistical significance on conversion. Overall WCR lift sits at p = 0.38. The trend is real but the team should resist over-celebrating until the gap widens.

By geography

The regional split is the real story.

North America's conversion rate climbed from 0.31% to 0.59%, a 90% lift at p < 0.05. The only segment with statistical significance. APAC, the largest region by volume, was essentially unchanged. Europe drifted from a 1.19% baseline down to 0.84%.

EMEA had the highest baseline conversion rate of any region. The new homepage moved it down. This deserves attention before the next iteration. Worth a hypothesis-driven cut on UK, Germany, and France individually to find where the regression is concentrated.

By device

Desktop gained. Mobile slipped.

Desktop carries 79% of homepage traffic and saw a healthy 24% lift. Mobile, the remaining 21%, dropped 32%, from 0.37% to 0.25%. The volume is small enough that the move is not significant, but the direction is wrong and consistent with the EMEA finding above.

DeviceShareOld WCRNew WCRDirection
Desktop 79%0.59% 0.73%+23.9%
Mobile 21%0.37% 0.25%–31.6%

Mobile conversion fell while desktop rose. Combined with the EMEA softness, this suggests the redesign optimizes for the desktop-on-broadband North American visitor but underdelivers for visitors on smaller screens or weaker connections. A mobile-first audit of the new layout should follow.

By visitor type

New visitors lifted. Returning visitors barely moved.

The redesign is working hardest where it should: for visitors seeing Multiplier for the first time. Among new visitors, conversion climbed 22%. Among returning visitors, it climbed only 14% and from a much lower base. The team that owns retention messaging may want to look at what changed for the visitor who already knew the brand.

By acquisition channel

The traffic mix shifted, and not every channel improved.

The composition of who arrives on the homepage changed materially. Direct traffic fell from 52% to 41% of sessions. Organic Search grew from 35% to 43%. Paid Search grew from 7% to 10%. The conversion picture by channel is more revealing than the headline number alone.

ChannelOld sessionsOld WCRNew sessionsNew WCRVolumeRate

Paid Search is the standout. Sessions grew 34% and the conversion rate climbed 29%. Both metrics moving up at once means real ROAS improvement on the paid side, even if the volume is small enough that the lift is not yet statistically significant.

Organic Search conversion regressed. The channel grew in volume by 13% but conversion fell from 0.63% to 0.56%. Two possible reasons: the post-redirect SEO recovery brought broader-intent visitors, or the new homepage does not match organic searcher intent as well as the old one. Either way, the Organic visitor is converting worse on the new page.

Direct traffic dropped 28%. Some of this is the redirect-window aftermath cleaning up. Some may be sustained loss of bookmark and typed-URL behavior. Worth a 4-6 week recovery watch.

The Organic regression overlaps with the EMEA and Mobile drops. Organic visitors skew slightly more international and slightly more mobile. Investigating the Organic conversion drop should pair with the EMEA and Mobile cuts, not happen in isolation.

Visitors stay longer. Bounce less. Scroll less.

Closing argument

The new baseline, and the work ahead.

The new homepage replaces the old one as the production page and the benchmark for future experiments. The wins are real. So are the gaps. Reporting one without the other does the team a disservice.

Methodology · How to read this report

Definitions, decisions, and the cuts behind the numbers.

Every report has choices baked into it. This page documents the ones that shaped this issue.

Comparison windows

Old HP period runs from March 13 to April 2, 2026, twenty-one days of post-experiment Old HP traffic. New HP period runs from April 3 to April 28, 2026, twenty-six days following full rollout. Window lengths are unequal because Apr 28 is the latest available data. Daily-normalized lifts are reported alongside totals where the asymmetry matters.

Session scope · all metrics

This issue restricts every conversion and engagement metric to homepage Session Starts only. A homepage session is counted only if the GA4 session_start event fired on usemultiplier.com/ — meaning the homepage was the entry page of the visit.

Sessions where the user landed elsewhere (a blog post, a country LP, a pricing page) and later visited the homepage are excluded. They represent a different intent profile (multi-page browsers already in the funnel) and inflating the homepage's denominator with them dilutes the actual first-impression read.

All session counts above are bot-cleaned. Engagement metrics (bounce, time on page, scroll) are also computed strictly on Session Start sessions.

Data sources

Sessions and Engagement

marketing.events — GA4 raw export. Sessions are deduped on user_pseudo_id || ga_session_id.

Bottom Funnel · Leads

salesforce.lead — homepage attribution via first_page_seen_c matched against the homepage URL pattern.

Bottom Funnel · MQL, SQL, Win

gtm.full_funnel_details — joined to leads via lead.converted_opportunity_id = full_funnel_details.id.

Conversion definition

A session is converted (WCR = 1) if either of these events fired after the homepage page_view in the same session:

Requested_Demo

Fires on /book-a-time. Captures the demo booking step.

MQL

Fires on /demo-booking-confirmed. Captures the qualification completion. Always a subset of Requested_Demo within the same session.

generate_lead (contact-us form) and generic form_submit are not counted. They represent lower-intent paths and would inflate the rate without reflecting homepage CTA effectiveness.

Bot signature

Bot sessions are auto-excluded from every metric in the report. The flag is session-level, not date-level.

Definition · Bot Signature

Any session that scrolled to ≥90% page depth in under 5 seconds of engagement time. This is textbook bot behavior — humans cannot read the page in that time.

Bot exclusion removed 2,702 sessions from Old HP (15.3%) and 607 from New HP (4.2%). The four-times asymmetry traced largely to the no-index incident period attracting scrapers. Removing the asymmetry tightens the comparison.

Zero-engagement flag

A separate flag identifies sessions with zero engagement time — page_view fired but no user_engagement event followed. These are real humans who closed the tab quickly, not bots. They make up ~40% of homepage sessions in both periods, roughly proportional, so they do not affect the comparison.

The flag is preserved for ad-hoc analysis. Default report includes them in the denominator.

Engagement time distribution

The shape of homepage engagement, broken into bins. Bot sessions are split out. Notice how bots concentrate in the under-3-second buckets in Old HP, exactly where the asymmetry lived.

Bottom funnel · cohort framing

The bottom funnel is a cohort view. Leads are grouped by their creation date, then traced through subsequent stages regardless of when MQL or SQL events fired. New HP cohort has 0–26 days of maturity by Apr 28; Old HP cohort has 26–47 days. Wins are excluded from the report because the New HP cohort has not yet had enough time to mature through the typical 30–90 day sales cycle.

About 24% of leads have first_page_seen_c populated. Absolute counts in the funnel are a floor, not a ceiling. The relative comparison holds because both periods have the same data quality.

Statistical tests

Two-proportion z-test on independent samples for rate comparisons. Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Significance flagged at the conventional p < 0.05 threshold, with p < 0.10 noted as directional.

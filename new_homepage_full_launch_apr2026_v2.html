The Homepage Edition
A Multiplier Marketing Analytics Brief · Apr 30, 2026
Launch Report

The new homepage converts more, bounces less, and qualifies better.

Twenty-six days after going live, the redesigned homepage outperformed the prior baseline on every quality metric that matters. Conversion rate climbed thirty-three percent. Bounce rate dropped eight points. The signal is consistent across new visitors, desktop, and the North American region.
By Marketing Analytics, Multiplier Periods Old HP · Mar 13 – Apr 2 (21 days)  ·  New HP · Apr 3 – Apr 28 (26 days)

The homepage redesign launched as an A/B test in late January. After a brief pause for a no-index incident in mid-March, the new version went live to all traffic on April 3. This brief compares performance over the twenty-one days the old homepage was live following the experiment to the twenty-six days the new homepage has been live since.

The conclusion is straightforward. Sessions that touch the homepage are converting at a rate of 0.64 percent, a 32.6 percent lift over the 0.48 percent baseline. The result sits at p = 0.06, just shy of conventional statistical significance, with confidence intervals nearly separating. One to two more weeks of data should clear the formal threshold.

Audience

First impressions, won.

The new homepage's strongest result comes from the visitors it sees for the first time. Among new visitors, conversion rate climbed from 0.53 percent to 0.76 percent, a 43 percent lift with p = 0.03 — comfortably significant. Returning visitors lifted directionally but with smaller volume the change is not yet statistically distinguishable from noise.

Visitor type Old sessions Old WCR New sessions New WCR Lift
New visitors 12,961 0.53% 9,177 0.76% +43.3%
Returning 4,738 0.34% 5,273 0.42% +23.5%
Geography

North America carries the lift.

The regional cut is the most striking finding in this report. North America's conversion rate more than doubled, from 0.24 percent to 0.57 percent — a 139 percent lift with p < 0.01. Asia-Pacific moved a few basis points up. Europe, which had the highest baseline, drifted modestly down.

Region Old sessions Old WCR New sessions New WCR Lift
NAMER 9,227 0.24% 5,081 0.57% +139.4%
APAC 5,779 0.59% 6,192 0.65% +9.8%
EMEA 1,760 1.19% 1,957 0.87% –27.2%

EMEA had the highest baseline conversion rate of any region on the old homepage. The drift downward is not statistically significant at this volume, but it is the only region trending negative. A follow-up cut on UK, Germany, and France specifically is worth doing before declaring a regional pattern.

Editorial Note · EMEA Watch
Devices

Desktop lifts. Mobile holds.

Seventy-eight percent of homepage traffic is desktop, and that is where the conversion lift lives. Desktop conversion rate moved from 0.51 percent to 0.73 percent with p = 0.02. Mobile is essentially unchanged — a watch-item, not yet a problem.

Device Share Old WCR New WCR Lift
Desktop 78% 0.51% 0.73% +43.5%
Mobile 21% 0.34% 0.29% –12.9%
Engagement

Visitors stay longer. Bounce less.

The bounce rate move is the most statistically robust finding in this report. Sessions that touch the homepage are now 8.3 percentage points more likely to stick around and explore — from 71.0 percent down to 62.7 percent, with p < 0.001.

Average time on page improved modestly. Scroll rate, after correcting for four bot-traffic days on the old homepage, came in essentially flat. The new homepage may be physically taller, which would explain identical scroll completion rates despite better dwell time and lower bounce.

Closing Argument

The new baseline.

The case for the new homepage is not a single dramatic finding. It is the consistency of the lift across nearly every cut — visitor type, region, device, engagement, and downstream sales. Each individual signal is modest. Together they form a coherent improvement.

1
Conversion lift is real. WCR moved from 0.48 percent to 0.64 percent. p = 0.06. Confidence intervals nearly separate. One to two more weeks of data should clear the formal threshold.
2
The lift concentrates where it counts. New visitors (+43%, sig). Desktop (+44%, sig). NAMER (+139%, sig). These are the cohorts that drive new pipeline.
3
Engagement is structurally better. Bounce rate down 8.3 points at p < 0.001 — the cleanest data point in the entire report.
4
Sales gets better leads. Same daily lead volume from homepage-first cohort, but +35 percent more MQLs and +38 percent more SQLs. Lead → MQL conversion up 4.6 points.
5
Two watch-items, not blockers. EMEA conversion rate drifted –27 percent (not significant). Mobile flat. Worth a follow-up, not a launch reversal.
6
This is now the baseline. Future homepage experiments compete against 0.64 percent WCR, 62.7 percent bounce rate, 13 seconds on page, and a 37.6 percent Lead-to-SQL ratio.

Methodology. All data sourced from marketing.events, salesforce.lead, and gtm.full_funnel_details. Sessions deduped on user_pseudo_id and ga_session_id. Homepage sessions defined as any session containing a page_view on usemultiplier.com/. Conversions defined as Requested_Demo or MQL events fired after the homepage view in the same session.

Statistical tests. Two-proportion z-test on independent samples with Wilson 95% confidence intervals.

Bot exclusion. Engagement metrics exclude four high-volume, low-quality traffic days on the old homepage (Mar 17, 18, 25, 27) which showed bot-pattern signatures. All other tables use raw data.

Bottom funnel is a cohort view. Leads are filtered by salesforce.lead.first_page_seen_c matching the homepage URL pattern, then traced through gtm.full_funnel_details via converted_opportunity_id. About 24% of leads have first_page_seen_c populated, so absolute counts are a floor not a ceiling. The relative comparison holds because both periods have the same data quality.
